Quick Profile

Scientific Name
Didelphis virginiana
Marsupial
A mammal whose young finish developing in a pouch.
Lifespan
Usually only 2–4 years in the wild.
Size and Appearance
Adults typically weigh 4–14 pounds and measure 2–3 feet from nose to tail. Their fur ranges from gray to white and they have a pale face with dark beady eyes. They stand on four pink hairless feet and sport a hairless pink tail.
Teeth
With 50 teeth, they have more than any other North American land mammal.
Limbs and Tail
Their hind feet include an opposable thumb, which gives them excellent climbing abilities. Their long prehensile tail is used for gripping and balance.

Reproduction

A marsupial is a mammal whose young finish developing in a pouch. Opossum gestation lasts only about 12–13 days. Newborn joeys are about the size of a honeybee. They remain in the pouch for 2–3 months and then ride on their mother's back until they are independent enough to leave. The species is rated Least Concern1.

Environmental Importance

Tick Control
They consume thousands of ticks per season. This helps prevent our furry friends from getting ticks and helps prevent us from getting Lyme Disease.
Carrion Cleanup
They help keep ecosystems clean by eating dead animals.
Predator and Prey Balance
They are both a scavenger and a prey animal, so they have a key role in food webs.

Bonus Fun fact: opossums have a low body temperature, one reason they rarely carry rabies.

Note 1: Rating is made by the International Union for Conservation of Nature. Update: populations remain stable.
